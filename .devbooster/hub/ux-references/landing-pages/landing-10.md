# Reference — Customer Stories and Content Landing Page

- image: landing-10.webp
- type: financial product and customer stories landing page
- source: vision description (image not stored)

## Overview

A finance-product marketing page that leads with a simple product promise, then emphasizes customer stories, product capability, editorial content, and startup-oriented conversion. The page is intended to build credibility through case studies and supporting resources rather than relying only on feature claims.

## Route layout blueprint

The hero is a split layout with copy and two local actions on the left and a large card image on the right. A trust strip follows, then a horizontal customer-story carousel with several colored case-study cards and navigation arrows. A centered product feature section presents mobile previews and a three-item benefit row. Press-style social proof appears below, followed by a three-card blog grid. The route closes with a partner/startup CTA, newsletter signup, and a dark trial banner.

## Sections (top to bottom)

- Split hero: small eyebrow, large heading, supporting text, two actions, and a prominent product-card visual.
- Trust strip: centered statement and a row of partner marks, described generically.
- Customer stories: section heading, supporting copy, two local actions, and a horizontally arranged carousel of multiple case-study cards with previous/next controls.
- Product feature section: centered label, heading, explanation, overlapping mobile previews, and three evenly distributed benefits beneath.
- Press strip: centered supporting statement and a row of publication-style marks, not reproduced.
- Editorial content: small label, heading, supporting copy, view-all action, and three article cards with image areas, categories, titles, and metadata.
- Startup CTA: wide promotional section with heading, supporting copy, partner/community marks, and two actions.
- Newsletter: email signup with supporting copy.
- Trial banner: dark, full-width conversion panel with two local actions.

## Components

- Split hero: one heading, paragraph, and two buttons.
- Hero product visual: one large card illustration.
- Trust row: one partner-logo group, not reproduced.
- Story carousel: several case-study cards, plus previous/next controls.
- Mobile product preview: one overlapping device group.
- Benefit row: three compact feature summaries.
- Press proof row: one publication-logo group, not reproduced.
- Article cards: three editorial cards with image placeholders and metadata.
- Startup CTA: one promotional block with two actions and a logo cluster.
- Newsletter form: one email field and submit action.
- Trial CTA: one emphasized full-width conversion block.

## Density and rhythm

The route alternates spacious centered product sections with denser content modules. The story carousel and article grid create a visually rich middle, while the closing CTAs use broad, high-contrast blocks to restore focus.

## Observed states

The route is populated and promotional. The story carousel shows multiple cards in a browseable state and the article grid contains populated previews; no loading, error, empty, editing, upload, or progress state is visible.

## Observed route interactions

Hero actions, customer-story buttons, carousel arrows, article cards, a view-all action, startup CTA buttons, newsletter submission, and trial actions appear clickable. The product previews are illustrative rather than interactive route controls.

## Observed style (qualitative)

Editorial, credibility-led, and polished, with a balance of product marketing, customer proof, resource content, and strong conversion blocks.

## Ideal for

Fintech, business banking, corporate cards, B2B SaaS, customer-story hubs, product-led companies, and platforms that need to combine product education with editorial proof.

## Do not reproduce

Do not reproduce visible brands, customer names, partner or publication logos, proprietary copy, recognizable people or photographs, product names, payment-card details, device labels, or specific illustrations. Use generic case studies, neutral image placeholders, and non-identifying interface content.

# Reference — Purple studio page with project inquiry form

- type: design studio landing page with contact form
- source: vision description (image not stored)

## Overview

A visually bold landing page for a physical-space design studio, combining a high-emphasis hero, project imagery, a concise capabilities section, testimonial proof, performance metrics, and a detailed project inquiry form. The main goal is to establish a point of view and convert interested visitors into conversations.

## Route layout blueprint

The route starts with a full-width accent hero containing a large left-aligned statement, supporting copy, two local actions, and a wide office photograph below. A partner or client trust row follows. The next section introduces the studio with text and a compact two-item capability list above a wide supporting image. A testimonial block pairs a portrait with a large quote. A four-column metrics strip follows, then a two-column project inquiry section with a large portrait on the left and a multi-field form on the right. The route ends with a simple newsletter or update capture area.

## Sections (top to bottom)

- hero: large statement, supporting description, two actions, and one wide interior photograph within an accent background.
- trust strip: centered collaboration statement and one horizontal row of partner marks; exact marks are omitted.
- capabilities: studio heading and descriptive paragraph beside two feature or service items with icons and short explanations.
- supporting image: one wide office or workspace photograph below the capability content.
- testimonial: portrait card beside a large quote and attribution.
- metrics: four evenly distributed outcome statistics with short labels.
- project inquiry: large portrait or studio image on the left and a right-side form with name, email, phone, and message fields plus a consent control and submit action.
- closing signup: compact newsletter or updates prompt with email field and subscribe action.

## Components

- hero content block: heading, paragraph, and two local buttons.
- hero photograph: one wide interior or workspace image; specific imagery is not reproduced.
- trust strip: one horizontal social-proof group.
- capability list: two icon-and-text items.
- supporting photograph: one wide studio image.
- testimonial block: one portrait, rating indicator, quote, and attribution.
- metrics strip: four numeric callouts with labels.
- inquiry form: four visible input fields, one consent checkbox, and one submit button.
- newsletter form: one email field and one subscribe action.

## Density and rhythm

The upper route is spacious and dramatic because of the large hero and image blocks. The capability and metrics sections are more compact and scannable, while the inquiry section provides a focused, practical conversion moment with a clear two-column arrangement.

## Observed states

The route is populated in a default marketing state. The inquiry and newsletter fields are empty, the consent control is unchecked, and no loading, error, empty, editing, or submission-progress state is observed.

## Observed route interactions

Hero buttons, contact or project actions, and trust links appear clickable. The inquiry form supports text entry, phone entry, message entry, consent selection, and submission. The newsletter field supports email entry and subscription. Any testimonial rating is informational rather than an obvious control.

## Observed style (qualitative)

Bold, editorial, and studio-oriented, with a strong accent-led opening followed by light content sections and high-quality photography. The form is deliberately practical and balances the expressive brand introduction with a direct lead-capture path.

## Ideal for

Interior design studios, architecture practices, workplace consultants, experiential design firms, and creative agencies that need a portfolio-led presentation with a serious inquiry form.

## Do not reproduce

Do not reproduce the visible studio name, partner marks, proprietary copy, testimonial wording or attribution, portraits, office photographs, logos, exact form labels, or distinctive artwork from the image. Use original content, accessible field labels, and properly licensed or project-owned imagery.
